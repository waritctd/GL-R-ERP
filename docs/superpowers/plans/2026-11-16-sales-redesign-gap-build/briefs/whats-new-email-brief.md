## Background
GL&R ERP is an HR + sales portal (React frontend on Vercel from `main`, Spring Boot backend on an on-prem server, Postgres). It has a staff-facing "มีอะไรใหม่" page with per-department release notices; CEO/HR can publish a notice to the in-app bell and, separately, email it. On 2026-11-16 the first notice was published (33 bell rows) and its email was attempted and failed for all 28 recipients because the Resend API key was invalid; the audit log nonetheless records them as emailed. A marker-based reset was built, reviewed and deployed (PR #1313) but not run. A parallel session ("Sales operations workflow redesign") is building and releasing the sales redesign and a second notice (`2026-11-17`); it owns the release content files and the releases to `main`. The owner prefers that sessions decide design questions themselves and record the reasoning, but production deploys, production database writes and emails to staff each need one explicit go from the owner. The owner works mostly from a phone, so keep questions short and self-contained.

## What to work out and then do
1. **Decide what "send it out" means in the product and state it before building.** Today publishing (bell + red dot) and emailing are two separate CEO/HR buttons, deliberately: `publish` never emails. The simplest faithful reading of the owner's instruction: when a CEO/HR user publishes a release, the email goes out as part of that same action (one press, one confirm that says how many people get a bell notice and how many get an email), still at-most-once per employee per release, still only to the roles each entry targets, still skipping people with no address. Keep the separate "ส่งอีเมล" action for people who joined or gained an address later. If you choose a different design, say why. Do not build an automatic send that fires on deploy with nobody pressing anything.
2. **Find out whether email works on production at all, read-only first.** The key in the on-prem `.env` was replaced on the evening of 2026-11-16 and the backend restarted, but no email has been observed to send since, and the sender `notifications@glr-portal.com` has not been confirmed as verified under the new key. Check the backend log for any send since the restart, successful or failed, and report what you find. If nothing has been sent, propose the smallest safe proof (one email to the owner's own address through an existing path) and ASK THE OWNER before sending it.
3. **The first notice (`2026-11-16`) was never delivered** although 28 `RELEASE_NOTE_EMAILED` audit rows say it was. `hr.audit_log` is append-only; the lookup honours a `RELEASE_NOTE_EMAIL_RESET` marker row; the exact operator SQL, with the advisory lock it must take, is in `email-reset-2026-11-16.sql` and in the Javadoc of `ReleaseNoticeRepository`. It has NOT been run. Running it and re-sending is a mass email to staff: ASK THE OWNER before doing either, and only after step 2 has shown an email really arrives.
4. **Do not edit** files under `frontend/src/features/whatsNew/releases/` or `frontend/public/whats-new/` (the other session owns them). Your change must work for any release, not one id.
5. **Hardening the review already called for:** the email action records "emailed" before the send is attempted and never learns that it failed, which is how 28 false rows were written. Make a failed send visible and recoverable without a database operator: record the failure per employee (an append-only audit row from the async failure path is the approach the reviewer suggested) so the action can retry exactly those people and never re-mail someone whose email went out. Show CEO/HR the real outcome (sent / failed / no address) instead of an optimistic count.

6. **What the email says (owner, verbatim: "also give them the link and have a short brief what changed").** Each email carries (a) the link to that release's page for the reader, `https://glr-hub.com/whats-new/<releaseId>`, as a button and as plain text, and (b) a short summary of what changed FOR THAT READER'S DEPARTMENT: the titles of the entries their role gets, each with its one-sentence "why", a handful of lines in total, in Thai. No screenshots and no attachment; the page has the steps. Today's body (`ReleaseNoticePolicy.emailBody`) lists titles and the link; check what it really renders, extend it to include each entry's one-line summary, keep it readable on a phone, and keep a plain-text alternative. The summary text comes from the release content the frontend already sends in the publish/email request (see `publishPayload` in `audiences.js`), so decide whether the request needs to carry the one-line "why" per entry and, if it does, state that as a contract change, validate its length server-side, and escape it properly in the HTML (it is user-influenced input reaching an email). A reader must never be told about an entry their role cannot open.
